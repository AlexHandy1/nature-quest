Your Retiro Walk — A Nature Quest

# A Journey Through Retiro Park: Five Species Discovered

As morning light filters through the park's ancient trees, our expedition into Retiro's embrace begins with a stroke of fortune. Here, perched boldly on a branch, is the black-billed magpie—a striking study in monochromatic elegance. Its black-and-white plumage seems almost sculpted, yet watch closely: hints of iridescent blue and blue-green shimmer beneath the darkness, a hidden treasure visible only to patient eyes. Once confused with its European cousins, this remarkable North American corvid was not recognized as a distinct species until the year 2000—a reminder that even the boldest among us can hide secrets.

Moving deeper into the wooded heart of the park, our ears must now guide us. There—listen. A distinctive hiss-like call echoes through the branches. The Iberian green woodpecker, a medium-sized architect of bark and wood, appears before us. This creature exists nowhere else on Earth save the Iberian Peninsula, a living definition of endemism. Note the subtle differences from its European relatives: less black upon the head, a dusky eye-ring lending an air of knowing contemplation. Here is a bird that belongs utterly to this corner of the world.

Our path now leads toward water, where the park's wetlands hold treasures from distant continents. An Egyptian goose floats with serene grace—an African wanderer that traveled across oceans through human hands as an ornament, yet thrives here as if destined for this lake. Ancients once painted its likeness on temple walls; today it drifts before us, a living bridge to pharaonic Egypt.

Yet the water holds greater wonders. The black swan glides past—entirely obsidian save for a crimson bill like a brushstroke of fire. From Australia's remote wilderness, these monogamous nomads follow mysterious compulsions across the globe, their erratic migrations guided by climate and instinct.

Finally, as if to ground us in the familiar, the common mallard appears: humble, prolific, social. These dabbling ducks gather in their characteristic flocks, feeding gently on aquatic plants and small animals. In these everyday congregations lies a profound truth—that even urban sanctuaries remain places where the wild endures and thrives.
