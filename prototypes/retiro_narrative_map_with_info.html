Your Retiro Walk — A Nature Quest

Ah... Retiro Park. Madrid's great green lung, its gravel paths still cool with morning shade, awaits us. And here — barely past the gate — our first encounter. A flash of black and white against the Spanish sky. The Black-billed Magpie. Watch closely, for what appears at first to be simple monochrome plumage reveals, in the right light, a shimmer of iridescent blue and blue-green along the wing and that extraordinary tail. A member of the crow family, and like all corvids, formidably intelligent — adaptable enough to thrive here, thousands of miles from its native range across the western reaches of North America. Indeed, it was only in the year 2000 that science recognised this bird as a species entirely its own, distinct from its Eurasian cousin. A humble beginning to our walk — and already, a lesson in how much remains to be discovered.

We move on, deeper into the dappled woodland, and now we listen — for our next subject reveals itself first through sound. A curious, sibilant hiss echoes among the trees. The Iberian Green Woodpecker, found nowhere else on Earth but this very peninsula. Look for the telltale dusky ring around its eye — softer, less severe than its European relative's black mask — the subtle mark of a species that only recently earned its own name.

The path bends now toward water, and the character of our journey shifts entirely. Here, upon the park's great lake, we find the Egyptian Goose, a traveller from Africa, its warm brown eye-patches and rust-toned chest catching the sun. Beside it — or perhaps at a distance, for they keep their own company — drifts the Black Swan, midnight-dark and utterly serene, its blood-red bill cutting through still water. A native of far-off Australia, nomadic by nature, bonded for life to a single mate.

And so we complete our circuit where the water gathers quietly at its edge — the Common Mallard, humble, sociable, utterly at home. Our journey through Retiro's hidden wilderness draws, gently, to its close.
